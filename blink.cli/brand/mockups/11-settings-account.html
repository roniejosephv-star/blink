What "calm" means here

The amber accent is the most saturated color in the brand, and it is used twice on this page: on the plan card's tier badge, and on the "Tip" callouts in the rail. The "Upgrade" CTA stays cyan. Settings is not a sales page — it is a tool.

Why the danger zone is its own card

A red 1-px border, an amber warning icon, and a single button. No red body copy. The card lives at the bottom of the page — it is the last thing the eye lands on. The confirmation modal (not shown) types the account name to confirm. The soft-delete grace period is shown in the copy.

The plan card's job

Tell the user what they have and when it renews. The three small stat tiles (plugin projects, storage, CI minutes) are the "are you using it?" hook — if any of them is empty, the user is overpaying. The "Cancel plan" link is in red, low-saturation, last in the row.

The activity rail

Forensic, not social. The last blocked login (Singapore, 14 days ago) is shown in red so the user knows they were protected. The "this device" marker on the top row is the only positive social signal — it tells the user they are where they expect to be.
